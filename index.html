Training is the part everyone sees. Fueling is the part that decides whether that training turns into speed. This is a plain-language guide to eating and drinking around the pool — built for female swimmers, from morning practice to finals night.

Explore the guide

What this is for

Off The Block is a fueling reference for female swimmers and the people who coach them. Most nutrition advice is written for runners, lifters, or nobody in particular — it rarely accounts for a sport where you train twice a day, can't drink mid-set, sweat without noticing it, and race five times before lunch.

The guide is organised the way a swimmer's week actually runs. One section covers the training day — before, during, and after practice, including 5am wake-ups and doubles. The other covers competition — the night before, the gap between heats, and recovery across a three-day meet. A third section covers the vitamins and minerals female athletes most often run short on, why swimming specifically raises the risk, and how to work around it with food. The fueling log lets you record what you ate, how much you drank, and how you felt, so patterns show up over a season instead of getting forgotten by Thursday.

Everything here is educational. It is meant to give you a starting point and better questions — not to replace a doctor, a registered dietitian, or a blood test.

Off The Block is an educational resource, not medical or clinical advice. Nutrition needs vary with body size, training load, age, and health history. Talk to a doctor or a registered sports dietitian before starting a supplement, and go to one promptly if you notice missed periods, unexplained fatigue, or repeated injuries.
