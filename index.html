A fueling guide built for female swimmers — before, during, and after the water, plus the vitamins and minerals that keep you strong.

Pre-Training & Pre-Competition Fueling

3-4 hours before

Full Meal

  • Carb-focused base: oatmeal, rice, pasta, or toast — this is what fills muscle glycogen tanks
  • Moderate protein: eggs, greek yogurt, chicken
  • Low fat & low fiber to avoid a heavy stomach in the water
  • Hydrate steadily: 16-20oz water in this window
60-90 min before

Smaller Snack

  • Banana + honey, a bagel, granola bar, or a smoothie
  • Keep it mostly carbs — simple sugar is fine here, it digests fast
  • Avoid new/untested foods before competition
15-30 min before

Race-Day Top Off

  • Small carb hit if needed: a few dates, applesauce pouch, or sports drink
  • Sip water — don't chug right before you dive in

During Training & Competition

Sessions under 60 min

Water is usually enough

  • Sip between sets, don't wait until you feel thirsty
Sessions over 60-90 min / multi-event meets

Add carbs + electrolytes

  • Sports drink, diluted juice, or electrolyte tabs between heats
  • Simple carbs between events: fruit chews, banana, a small sports gel
  • Sodium matters — swimmers sweat more than people think, even in the pool
Meet-day rhythm

Between heats

  • Small, frequent carb snacks rather than one big meal
  • Keep hydrating consistently through the day, not just before your race

Post-Training & Post-Competition Recovery

Within 30-45 min

The recovery window

  • Carbs + protein together, roughly 3:1 ratio — chocolate milk is a classic for a reason
  • Rehydrate with fluid + sodium to replace sweat losses (weigh yourself before/after if you want to get precise)
Within 2 hours

Full recovery meal

  • Balanced plate: protein (chicken, fish, tofu, eggs), carbs (rice, potatoes, bread), veggies/fruit, healthy fat
  • This is where glycogen refilling and muscle repair really happen
Night before next session

Don't skip the evening meal

  • Consistent recovery day to day matters more than any single perfect meal

Vitamins & Minerals for Female Athletes

Female swimmers are at higher risk for a few specific deficiencies because of training volume, menstrual cycle losses, and sometimes under-fueling. Here's what to watch and how to work around it.

Iron

Most common deficiency in female athletes

  • Why: menstrual blood loss + "foot-strike" style hemolysis from hard training reduces iron stores
  • Signs of low iron: fatigue beyond normal, poor recovery, feeling constantly flat in the water
  • Food sources: red meat, poultry, beans, lentils, spinach, fortified cereal
  • Work-around: pair plant iron sources with vitamin C (citrus, peppers) to boost absorption; avoid tea/coffee right with iron-rich meals since it blocks uptake
  • Get ferritin checked with a doctor if fatigue is unexplained — don't self-supplement iron without a blood test, too much is harmful
Calcium & Vitamin D

Bone health duo

  • Why it matters: swimming is non-weight-bearing, so it doesn't build bone density the way running does — bone health has to come from nutrition
  • Food sources for calcium: dairy, fortified plant milk, tofu, leafy greens, almonds
  • Vitamin D: sunlight exposure, fatty fish, egg yolks, fortified foods — indoor pool athletes often run low, worth checking levels
  • Together they protect against stress fractures and support the hormonal health tied to strong training
B Vitamins

Energy metabolism

  • B12 and folate support red blood cell production — extra important alongside iron
  • Food sources: eggs, dairy, meat, fortified cereals; B12 needs extra attention for anyone eating plant-based
Watch for: RED-S

Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport

  • Happens when calorie intake doesn't match training demands — can affect periods, bone density, mood, and performance all at once
  • Signs: missed or irregular periods, constant fatigue, frequent injuries, mood changes
  • This is a "talk to a doctor or sports dietitian" flag, not a diet-tweak-at-home flag

This guide is educational, not medical advice — for personalized supplement or deficiency guidance, check in with a doctor or registered sports dietitian.
